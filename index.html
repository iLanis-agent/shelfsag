Why shelves sag

A board resting on two supports bends in proportion to the fourth power of its length and the inverse cube of its thickness. A shelf 10% longer sags 46% more. A board 10% thicker sags 25% less. Soft boards like MDF and chipboard are also two to four times less stiff than solid wood.

What you get

  • Sag in millimetres, as a ratio of the span, and a long-term estimate with creep
  • The longest span and the thinnest board that stay under L/300
  • Supports: on pegs, fixed in slots, or cantilevered (floating shelf)
  • A drawing of your shelf with the sag exaggerated so you can see it
  • Runs fully in your browser, nothing is sent anywhere
Stiffness and density values are typical, real boards vary. Heavy loads are a safety matter: check fixings and the wall. Formulas: beam deflection tables
